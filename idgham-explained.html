Idgham in Tajweed: Merging
Letters, Rules and Examples

Idgham (إدغام) is the merging of a noon sakinah or tanween into the next letter. It happens before six letters: ي ر م ل و ن.

What is idgham?

Idgham means "merging". When a noon sakinah (نْ) or tanween ends a word and one of the six letters begins the next word, you do not say the noon on its own. It blends into the next letter, which is pronounced as if it carried a shaddah. The six letters are remembered as يَرْمَلُونَ.

Say مَن يَقُولُ without a pause between the words. The noon dissolves into the ي and you hear one joined sound. That is idgham.

Two kinds: with and without ghunnah

Idgham with ghunnahي ن م وMerge and hold a nasal sound for about 2 countsمَن يَقُولُ · مِن نِّعْمَةٍ · مِن مَّاءٍ · مِن وَالٍ
Idgham without ghunnahل رMerge fully, no nasal soundمِن رَّبِّهِمْ · مِن لَّدُنْهُ · غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ

Teachers describe the merge with ghunnah as complete for ن م and incomplete for ي و, where a trace of the original letter's quality remains. Follow your teacher's terms.

The exception: same word

Idgham only applies across two words. When the noon sakinah and one of the letters sit in the same word, the noon is pronounced clearly. Examples are الدُّنْيَا (2:85) and صِنْوَانٌ (13:4). Teachers call this izhar mutlaq, and it is the most common place students merge by mistake.

How idgham should sound

✅
One joined sound

No pause between the two words. The second letter is doubled, as if it had a shaddah.

✅
Ghunnah only where it belongs

Hold about two counts for ي ن م و. For ل and ر, drop the nasal sound entirely.

💡
A quick check

If you can hear the noon as a separate sound before the next letter, you have not merged it.

Examples from the Quran

In مَن يَقُولُ (2:8) the noon merges into ي with ghunnah. In مِن نِّعْمَةٍ (16:53) it merges into ن, and in مِن مَّاءٍ (24:45) into م, both with ghunnah. In مِن وَالٍ (13:11) it merges into و with ghunnah.

In مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ (2:5) and مِّن لَّدُنْهُ (18:2) it merges into ر and ل without ghunnah. With tanween, غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ (2:173) merges the tanween into ر without ghunnah.

Common mistakes

❌
Pronouncing the noon

Saying the noon clearly and then the next letter. The two sounds must join.

❌
Ghunnah before ل and ر

Adding a nasal sound where there is none.

❌
No ghunnah before ي ن م و

Merging without the two-count nasal sound.

❌
Merging inside one word

Applying idgham to الدُّنْيَا or صِنْوَانٌ, where the noon must stay clear.

A simple idgham practice

Step 1: Say مَن يَقُولُ، مِن نِّعْمَةٍ، مِن مَّاءٍ slowly, holding the ghunnah for two counts. Step 2: Say مِن رَّبِّهِمْ and مِن لَّدُنْهُ with no nasal sound. Step 3: Say الدُّنْيَا and check that the noon stays clear. Record yourself and compare with a teacher or with QariAI.

Idgham FAQ

What is idgham in tajweed?

Idgham (إدغام) means merging. When a noon sakinah or tanween is followed by one of six letters at the start of the next word, the noon is not pronounced separately: it blends into the next letter, which carries a shaddah.

What are the letters of idgham?

Six letters: ي ر م ل و ن, remembered as يَرْمَلُونَ. They are split into two groups: ي ن م و merge with ghunnah, and ل ر merge without ghunnah.

What is the difference between idgham with and without ghunnah?

With ghunnah (ي ن م و), the merge keeps a nasal sound held for about two counts, as in مَن يَقُولُ. Without ghunnah (ل ر), the noon disappears fully with no nasal sound, as in مِن رَّبِّهِمْ.

Does idgham apply when the noon and the letter are in the same word?

No. Idgham needs the noon sakinah at the end of one word and the idgham letter at the start of the next. In words such as الدُّنْيَا and صِنْوَانٌ the noon is pronounced clearly. This is called izhar mutlaq.

Does idgham work with tanween?

Yes. Tanween behaves like a noon sakinah, so غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ merges the tanween into the ر without ghunnah.

What is the difference between idgham and ikhfa?

In idgham the noon merges into the next letter, which is doubled. In ikhfa the noon is hidden before one of 15 other letters but not merged, and it keeps a ghunnah with the tongue held near the letter.

How do I practise idgham?

Say مَن يَقُولُ، مِن نِّعْمَةٍ، مِن رَّبِّهِمْ slowly, joining the two words with no pause. Hold the ghunnah for two counts in the first two, and drop it in the third. Record yourself and compare with a teacher or with QariAI.

Idgham quick reference

  • Trigger: noon sakinah or tanween at the end of a word.
  • Letters: ي ر م ل و ن (يَرْمَلُونَ) at the start of the next word.
  • With ghunnah: ي ن م و, about two counts.
  • Without ghunnah: ل ر.
  • Exception: same word, so no idgham.
