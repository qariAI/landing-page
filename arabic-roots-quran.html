Quranic Arabic · Vocabulary Guide

Arabic Roots in the Qur’an: Learn Vocabulary by Root

Most Arabic words grow from a short root that carries a core meaning. Learn the roots and you learn families of words at once. This guide explains how roots work and lists 139 of the most useful Quranic roots, grouped by meaning, with their key words and how often they occur. When you are ready to practise, the free Arabic Roots app turns them into flashcards, quizzes and word-building games.

How Arabic roots work

A root is usually three consonants, called radicals, that hold a core idea. A pattern (wazn) then adds vowels and extra letters to shape that idea into a noun, verb or participle. Take the root ك ت ب (k-t-b), which carries the idea of writing:

WordReads asTypeMeaning
كِتَابkitābnounbook, scripture
كَتَبَkatabaverbhe wrote
كَاتِبkātibactive participlewriter
مَكْتُوبmaktūbpassive participlewritten
اِكْتَتَبَiktatabaverbhe subscribed, wrote down

Same three letters, different patterns, related meanings. Seeing that link is what makes vocabulary stick.

The 10 roots to learn first

These are the most frequent roots in this collection, ranked by how often they occur in the Qur’an, so you meet them constantly.

#1 · ʾ-l-h

أ ل ه — divinity, God, worship

Key words: اِللَّه (Allah, God) · إِلٰه (a deity, a god) · أَلَّهَ (he deified) · تَأَلَّهَ (he devoted himself to worship)

Memory hook: Ilah" sounds like "I la(w)" — the one whose law you live under. Same letters as الله Allah.

#2 · q-w-l

ق و ل — speaking, saying

Key words: قَالَ (he said) · قَوْل (a saying, speech) · قَائِل (one who says) · تَقَوَّلَ (he falsely ascribed a saying)

Memory hook: Call" — to call out words. Q-W-L ≈ "call": قال = he called out / said.

#3 · k-w-n

ك و ن — being, existing, becoming

Key words: كَانَ (he was, it was) · مَكَان (place, position) · مَكَانَة (standing, status) · كَوَّنَ (he formed, constituted)

Memory hook: Con" as in "be-come": K-W-N is "to be". كان = he was, يكون = he will be.

#4 · r-b-b

ر ب ب — lordship, nurturing

Key words: رَبّ (lord, master, sustainer) · رَبَّانِيّ (devoted scholar, godly teacher) · رَبَّى (he raised, nurtured) · تَرْبِيَة (upbringing, education)

Memory hook: Rub" — a lord rubs and nurtures you along, like a gardener tending a plant. رب = lord, ربّى = raised.

#5 · ʾ-m-n

أ م ن — safety, faith, trust

Key words: آمَنَ (he believed) · مُؤْمِن (believer) · إِيمَان (faith, belief) · مُؤْمِنَة (female believer)

Memory hook: Amen" — a word you say when you trust; same root as امن (safe) and إيمان (faith).

#6 · ʿ-l-m

ع ل م — knowledge

Key words: عَلِمَ (he knew) · عَلِيم (all-knowing) · عَالَم (world, universe) · أَعْلَم (more knowing, most knowing)

Memory hook: Alum(ni)" / "ʿilm" — an alumnus is someone who has gone through learning. علم = knowledge.

#7 · q-w-m

ق و م — standing up, people

Key words: قَوْم (people, nation) · قِيامَة (resurrection, the Standing) · أَقَامَ (he established, performed (prayer)) · قَامَ (he stood up)

Memory hook: Come" and stand: a قوم is a people who stand up together. قام = he stood up.

#9 · ʾ-t-y

أ ت ي — coming, bringing

Key words: آتَى (he brought, gave) · إِيتَاء (giving (zakat)) · مُؤْتٍ (one who gives) · مَأْتِيّ (that which is come upon)

Memory hook: Attain" — to arrive and bring it. أتى = he came; آتى = he brought/gave.

#10 · k-f-r

ك ف ر — covering, disbelief, ingratitude

Key words: كَفَرَ (he disbelieved, was ungrateful) · كَافِر (disbeliever) · كَفُور (utterly ungrateful) · كَفّارَة (expiation (kaffāra))

Memory hook: Cover" — K-F-R and "cover" share the sound: a كافر covers the truth; a farmer covers seed (كافر also meant farmer).

#11 · b-y-n

ب ي ن — clarity, separation

Key words: مُبِين (clear, manifest) · بَيِّنَة (clear proof) · بَيَّنَ (he made clear) · تَبَيَّنَ (it became clear)

Memory hook: Between" — what is between two things is clear. بين = between, بيّن = clear.

How to learn roots effectively

  • Start with frequency. Ten common roots unlock far more of the text than ten rare ones.
  • Learn the root with two or three words. A root alone is abstract; كِتَاب and كَاتِب make it concrete.
  • Notice patterns. Once you know that فَاعِل gives a doer, new roots become predictable.
  • Review little and often. Flashcards and short quizzes beat long cramming sessions.
  • Hear it in recitation. Pair vocabulary with good recitation; see our beginner tajweed guide for pronunciation.

What are Arabic roots?

Most Arabic words are built from a root of usually three consonants that carries a core meaning. Patterns (called awzan) add vowels and extra letters around the root to make nouns, verbs and participles. For example, the root ك ت ب (k-t-b) carries the idea of writing and gives words such as كِتَاب (book) and كَاتِب (writer).

Do I need to learn roots to understand Quranic Arabic?

No, but it helps. Recognising a root lets you guess the meaning of unfamiliar words and see why related words belong together, which makes vocabulary easier to remember. It complements, and does not replace, learning grammar and reading with a qualified teacher.

What is a wazn (pattern)?

A wazn is a template, traditionally written with the root ف ع ل, that shows how a word is formed from a root. فَاعِل, for instance, usually gives the doer (كَاتِب, writer), while مَفْعُول gives the thing done (مَكْتُوب, written). The Arabic Roots app lists the patterns in use with examples.
