Izhar in Tajweed: Pronouncing the Noon Clearly
Letters, Rule and Examples

Izhar (إظهار) is pronouncing a noon sakinah or tanween clearly before six throat letters: ء هـ ع ح غ خ.

What is izhar?

ء
هـ
ع
ح
غ
خ

Izhar means "making clear". When a noon sakinah (نْ) or tanween is followed by one of these six letters, the noon is pronounced fully and cleanly. There is no merging as in idgham, no turning into a meem as in iqlab, and no hiding as in ikhfa. Because the six letters come from the throat (halq), the rule is called izhar halqi.

Say مِنْ هَادٍ slowly. You hear a clear noon, then the ه. No nasal hum is held between them. That is izhar.

How izhar sounds

✅
A clear noon

The tongue touches its place and the noon is pronounced fully.

✅
No held ghunnah

Do not hold the nasal sound for two counts. Move straight into the throat letter.

💡
A quick check

If you can feel a long nasal hum, you hid the noon as in ikhfa. Izhar is short and clean.

Examples from the Quran

Across two words: مَنْ آمَنَ (2:62), مِنْ هَادٍ (13:33), مِنْ عِلْمٍ (18:5), مِنْ غِلٍّ (7:43) and مِنْ خَيْرٍ (2:197). With tanween: عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ (2:32) and كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ (112:4). Inside one word: أَنْعَمْتَ (1:7) and يَنْهَوْنَ (3:104).

Common mistakes

❌
Adding ghunnah

Holding a nasal sound before the throat letter. Izhar has no ghunnah.

❌
Hiding the noon

Treating the noon like ikhfa instead of saying it clearly.

❌
Merging into the throat letter

Dropping the noon so the two sounds blur together.

❌
Rushing past the noon

Skipping the noon entirely. It must be heard.

A simple izhar practice

Step 1: Say مِنْ آمَنَ، مِنْ هَادٍ، مِنْ عِلْمٍ slowly. Step 2: Say مِنْ غِلٍّ، مِنْ خَيْرٍ، عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ. Step 3: Say أَنْعَمْتَ. Check each noon is clear with no held hum. Record yourself and compare with a teacher or with QariAI.

Izhar FAQ

What is izhar in tajweed?

Izhar (إظهار) means making clear. When a noon sakinah or tanween is followed by one of six throat letters, the noon is pronounced clearly and fully, with no ghunnah and no merging.

What are the six izhar letters?

ء هـ ع ح غ خ. They are throat letters, which is why this rule is called izhar halqi (throat izhar).

Why are these letters called throat letters?

They are produced in the throat (halq), far from the tongue-tip place where the noon is made. Because the two sounds are so far apart, the noon cannot merge or hide, so it stays clear.

Is there ghunnah in izhar?

No. The noon is pronounced clearly and briefly, with only its natural light nasal quality, and you move straight into the next letter. It is not held for two counts.

Does izhar apply with tanween?

Yes. Tanween follows the same rule as noon sakinah, so عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ and كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ have a clear tanween sound before the throat letter.

Does izhar apply inside one word?

Yes. In أَنْعَمْتَ (1:7) and يَنْهَوْنَ (3:104) the noon sakinah and the throat letter are in the same word, and the noon is pronounced clearly.

How do I practise izhar?

Say مِنْ آمَنَ، مِنْ هَادٍ، مِنْ عِلْمٍ، مِنْ حَكِيمٍ، مِنْ غِلٍّ، مِنْ خَيْرٍ slowly. Pronounce the noon fully, do not hold a nasal sound, then say the throat letter. Record yourself and compare with a teacher or with QariAI.

Izhar quick reference

  • Trigger: noon sakinah or tanween.
  • Letters: ء هـ ع ح غ خ (throat letters).
  • Sound: clear noon, no ghunnah.
  • Where: inside one word or across two words.
