What Is Qalqalah?
Meaning, Rules and Types in Tajweed

Qalqalah (قَلْقَلَة) is the short echo or bounce you hear on five Arabic letters — ق ط ب ج د — when they carry a sukoon. It is how a stopped letter is released clearly instead of being cut off.

Qalqalah in one minute

  • Meaning: “shaking” or “disturbance” — the sound of a closed letter being released.
  • Letters: Qaf, Ta, Ba, Jeem, Dal (ق ط ب ج د), remembered as قُطْبُ جَدٍّ (Qutbu Jadd).
  • When: only when the letter has a sukoon, written in the mushaf or created by stopping on it.
  • How it sounds: a brief bounce, never an added “a”, “i” or “u”.

What does qalqalah mean?

The Arabic word قَلْقَلَة comes from a root associated with shaking and disturbance. Tajweed teachers use it for a very specific sound: when a qalqalah letter is closed with a sukoon, the sound is held for an instant and then released with a slight vibration at the letter’s articulation point.

Say the English word “cup” and stop on the last sound without letting go. Then release your lips slightly. That small release is what qalqalah sounds like on a letter such as ب — except in Arabic it must not turn into a vowel.

Why these five letters?

Qaf, Ta, Ba, Jeem and Dal are all letters that close the airflow completely at their articulation point (stop consonants). When one of them has a sukoon, the closure is not followed by a vowel, so the letter would risk being swallowed or sounding abrupt. Qalqalah is the clear release that keeps it audible. Because each letter is made in a different place — the back of the tongue for Qaf, the tongue tip for Ta and Dal, the lips for Ba, the middle of the tongue for Jeem — the makharij chart is the best way to place the bounce correctly.

When does qalqalah apply?

✅
The letter has a sukoon

Inside a word, as in يَجْعَلُونَ (the Jeem), or أَبْتَرُ (the Ba).

✅
You stop on the letter

At the end of an ayah or when pausing, as in أَحَدْ (Al-Ikhlas) and الْفَلَقْ (Al-Falaq).

❌
The letter has a vowel

If it carries fatha, kasra or damma it is read with that vowel and there is no qalqalah.

Types of qalqalah in brief

Teachers usually describe a lighter bounce when the sakin letter is inside a word and a clearer bounce when you stop on it at the end. Some add a fullest level for a stopped letter with a shaddah. The labels differ between teachers, so follow your own, but the principle is the same: the more final the letter, the more audible the release. The three levels are laid out with examples in our letters of qalqalah guide.

Common beginner mistakes

❌
Cutting the letter off

No release at all, so the word ends abruptly.

❌
Adding a vowel

Turning the bounce into “ah”, “ih” or “uh”, which changes the word.

❌
Over-doing it

A loud, exaggerated bounce breaks the flow of recitation. Aim for a brief, controlled release.

How to practise

Step 1: Say أَقْ، أَطْ، أَبْ، أَجْ، أَدْ slowly. Step 2: Close the letter, then release it without adding a vowel. Step 3: Apply it to the Quran examples above, first slowly and then in the full ayah. Record yourself or use the free tajweed mistakes checker to hear whether the bounce is missing or over-done. Qalqalah FAQ

What is qalqalah in tajweed?

Qalqalah is the short echo or bounce heard on five Arabic letters (ق ط ب ج د) when they carry a sukoon, either inside a word or because you stop on them. It is a controlled release of the letter, not a vowel.

What does qalqalah mean in Arabic?

The word قَلْقَلَة comes from a root meaning to shake or to disturb. In tajweed it names the slight shaking of the sound when a stopped letter is released.

What are the qalqalah letters?

The qalqalah letters are Qaf, Ta, Ba, Jeem and Dal (ق ط ب ج د), remembered with the phrase قُطْبُ جَدٍّ (Qutbu Jadd). The full list with examples is in our guide to the letters of qalqalah.

When does qalqalah happen?

It happens when one of the five letters has a sukoon, written in the mushaf or created by stopping at the end of a word. If the letter carries a vowel, it is read with that vowel and there is no qalqalah.

What are the types of qalqalah?

Most teachers describe a lighter bounce on a sakin letter inside a word (minor) and a clearer bounce when you stop on the letter (major). Some also name a strongest level for a stopped shaddah. Follow your teacher’s terms.

Is qalqalah the same as adding a vowel?

No. Adding “a”, “i” or “u” after the letter changes the word. Qalqalah is only the release of the stopped sound.

How do I practise qalqalah?

Say أَقْ، أَطْ، أَبْ، أَجْ، أَدْ slowly, release each letter without a vowel, then apply the bounce to short Quran examples. Recording yourself or using a tajweed checker helps you hear a flat or over-done bounce.
