Ikhfa in Tajweed: Hiding the Noon
Letters, Rules and Examples

Ikhfa (إخفاء) is hiding a noon sakinah or tanween before 15 letters, with a ghunnah held for about two counts.

What is ikhfa?

Ikhfa means "hiding". When a noon sakinah (نْ) or tanween is followed by one of 15 letters, the noon is not pronounced clearly, and it is not merged either. It is hidden in the nose as a ghunnah while the tongue gets ready for the next letter without touching its place.

How ikhfa sounds

✅
A held nasal sound

About two counts of ghunnah, steady and not cut short.

✅
Tongue stays away

The tongue does not touch the roof of the mouth for the noon. It stays near the place of the next letter.

💡
A quick check

If your tongue taps the ridge behind your teeth, you pronounced a clear noon, not ikhfa.

Ikhfa, idgham, iqlab and izhar

RuleLettersWhat happens to the noon
Izhar6 throat letters ء هـ ع ح غ خPronounced clearly
Idgham6: ي ر م ل و نMerged into the next letter
IqlabبTurned into a meem
IkhfaThe other 15 lettersHidden with ghunnah

Together these four rules cover every letter that can follow a noon sakinah or tanween, so ikhfa is the "everything else" rule. Learn izhar, idgham and iqlab first and the 15 ikhfa letters become the default.

Examples from the Quran

Across two words: مِّن دُونِ اللَّهِ (2:23), مِن شَرِّ (113:2), عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ (107:5), مَن جَاءَ (6:160) and مِّن ذَكَرٍ (49:13).

Inside one word: كُنتُمْ (2:23) and أَنتُمْ. With tanween: جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي (2:25) and قَوْلًا ثَقِيلًا (73:5).

Common mistakes

❌
Pronouncing a clear noon

Touching the tongue to the roof of the mouth. The noon must be hidden.

❌
Cutting the ghunnah short

Dropping the nasal sound before two counts.

❌
Merging instead of hiding

Doubling the next letter as in idgham. Ikhfa does not use a shaddah sound.

❌
Skipping ikhfa inside a word

Treating كُنتُمْ or أَنتُمْ as clear. Ikhfa applies inside one word too.

A simple ikhfa practice

Step 1: Say مِن قَبْلُ slowly and hold the nasal sound for two counts. Step 2: Say أَنتُمْ and كُنتُمْ, keeping the tongue off the roof of the mouth. Step 3: Say مِن شَرِّ and عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ. Record yourself and compare with a teacher or with QariAI.

Ikhfa FAQ

What is ikhfa in tajweed?

Ikhfa (إخفاء) means hiding. When a noon sakinah or tanween is followed by one of 15 letters, the noon is neither pronounced fully clearly nor merged. It is hidden: the sound moves into the nose as a ghunnah, and the tongue prepares for the next letter without touching its place.

What are the 15 letters of ikhfa?

ت ث ج د ذ ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ف ق ك. A common way to remember them is the first letter of each word in a short verse: صِفْ ذَا ثَنَا كَمْ جَادَ شَخْصٌ قَدْ سَمَا دُمْ طَيِّبًا زِدْ فِي تُقًى ضَعْ ظَالِمًا.

How long is the ghunnah in ikhfa?

About two counts, the same as other ghunnah rules. Hold the nasal sound steadily, then move straight into the next letter.

Does ikhfa apply inside one word?

Yes. Unlike idgham, ikhfa works whether the noon and the letter are in one word or in two. أَنتُمْ and كُنتُمْ are inside one word, while مِن قَبْلُ spans two words.

What is the difference between ikhfa and idgham?

In idgham the noon merges into the next letter, which is doubled, and there are only six letters. In ikhfa the noon is hidden but not merged, and there are 15 letters. Both keep a ghunnah in the cases that carry one.

Does the ikhfa sound change with the next letter?

Slightly. The ghunnah is a little lighter before letters pronounced at the front of the mouth and a little fuller before heavy letters such as ص ض ط ظ ق. Your teacher will correct this by ear.

How do I practise ikhfa?

Say مِن قَبْلُ، أَنتُمْ، مِن شَرِّ slowly. Keep the tongue away from the roof of the mouth on the noon, hold the nasal sound for two counts, then say the next letter. Record yourself and compare with a teacher or with QariAI.

Ikhfa quick reference

  • Trigger: noon sakinah or tanween.
  • Letters: the 15 letters ت ث ج د ذ ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ف ق ك.
  • Sound: hidden noon with about two counts of ghunnah.
  • Where: inside one word or across two words.
