Best Apps for Hifz Memorization in 2026:
No Teacher Required

📖 10 min read· Published September 2026· We include our own app — and its limitations

Most Hifz students practice alone most of the time — a teacher isn't available at 5am or during a lunch-break revision session. This guide looks specifically at apps built to catch memory mistakes during unsupervised practice: what each one actually listens for, and what still requires a human.

What "Hifz Support" Actually Means in an App

A lot of Quran apps mention Hifz somewhere in their marketing. Very few actually listen to your recitation and check it against the text in real time. It's worth separating three different things apps in this space tend to offer, since they get bundled together:

  • Live memory checking — the app listens to you recite from memory and flags specific words you got wrong, skipped, or added.
  • Revision scheduling — the app tracks what you've memorized and reminds you when to revise it (spaced repetition), without necessarily listening to your recitation.
  • Tajweed correction — a related but different feature: checking pronunciation, not memory. A student can recite from perfect memory with poor Tajweed, or vice versa.

The apps below differ mainly in which of these three they actually do, and how well.

Which One Should You Use?

If you want an app that listens to your recitation and catches memory mistakes as you go, on Android: QariAI is currently the only option that scores memory and pronunciation separately rather than conflating the two.

If you're on iOS: Tarteel is the strongest option for live memory checking, with the caveat that its Tajweed feedback is word-level rather than rule-specific.

If you already get your recitation checked elsewhere and mainly need a revision schedule: Quranly's planning tools are purpose-built for that, without the overhead of a recitation-analysis feature you won't use.

The Honest Limit

None of these apps replace a teacher for verifying correct transmission (riwayah). What they can do is catch the specific, mechanical mistakes — a dropped word, a swapped word, a rushed madd — that go unnoticed in solo practice and would otherwise only surface the next time a human happens to be listening.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should an app for Hifz memorization actually check?

It should check memory accuracy separately from pronunciation — whether you recited the correct words, in the correct order, without omissions or substitutions — rather than folding memory slips into a general pronunciation score.

Can an app replace a Hifz teacher?

No. Apps can catch omissions, substitutions, and word-level mistakes during solo practice, but they can't verify correct transmission the way a qualified teacher with an ijazah can. Treat them as a daily supplement between teacher sessions, not a replacement.

Which apps track Hifz specifically, not just Tajweed?

QariAI, Tarteel, and Quranly all offer some form of Hifz tracking. QariAI and Tarteel listen to live recitation and flag memory errors as you recite; Quranly is primarily a scheduling and progress tool without live recitation analysis.
