    About Makharij

    Makharij are the specific points in the mouth, nose, and throat where Arabic letters are articulated.

    About Makharij

    Makharij al-Huruf (مخارج الحروف) refers to the specific points in the mouth, throat, and nasal cavity where each Arabic letter is articulated. Correct Makharij is foundational to Tajweed — pronouncing a letter from the wrong point of articulation can change its sound enough to alter meaning, particularly for similar-sounding letter pairs like Daad and Dhaa, or Qaaf and Kaaf.

    Classical Tajweed scholarship identifies five main articulation zones: the throat (الحلق), the tongue (اللسان) — which accounts for the largest number of letters — the lips (الشفتان), the open space of the mouth and throat (الجوف), and the nasal cavity (الخيشوم), which governs Ghunna.

    This chart is a reference tool, not a substitute for a qualified Tajweed teacher. Correct articulation requires hearing and feedback that only a teacher — or, for practice between lessons, AI-assisted tools — can provide. The audio playback in this tool uses your browser's built-in speech synthesis as a rough pronunciation guide; it is not a substitute for hearing a qualified reciter.

    Makharij FAQ

    How many makharij are there?

    Most teachers count 17 specific articulation points, grouped into five areas: the open space of the mouth and throat (al-jawf), the throat (al-halq), the tongue (al-lisan), the lips (ash-shafatan) and the nasal cavity (al-khayshum).

    What are the five main areas of makharij?

    Al-jawf (mouth and throat space), al-halq (throat), al-lisan (tongue), ash-shafatan (lips) and al-khayshum (nasal cavity).

    Which letters come from the throat?

    Six letters: ء and ه from the deepest throat, ع and ح from the middle, and غ and خ from the upper throat.

    What is the difference between makharij and sifat?

    Makharij is where a letter is pronounced. Sifat are its qualities, such as heavy or light, strong or gentle. Together they define the letter's sound.

    How do I practise makharij?

    Say each letter with a short vowel while paying attention to where it is made, then compare confusable pairs such as ح and ه, or ص and س. Use the diagram above and get feedback from a teacher or the free tajweed checker.
